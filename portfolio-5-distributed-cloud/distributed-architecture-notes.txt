 SwiftBank Distributed Database Architecture

 Proposed design
PostgreSQL is the authoritative relational database used by SwiftBank. Money transfers and account updates are sent to the primary. Subject to replication lag and a tested failover procedure, a standby can accept WAL modifications and facilitate read-only access or recovery planning.

Practical implementation
I used `pg_basebackup` to set up a different local PostgreSQL 18.6 physical standby. The standby used a different data directory and port 5433, while the current server continued to be the primary on port 5432. The standby was described by the primary as "streaming" with asynchronous replication. PostgreSQL rejected a standby write with SQLSTATE `25006` after a marker written to an isolated `p5_replication_demo` table appeared on the standby. This confirms read-only behavior and local streaming. I didn't advertise the test failover or standby.

Design considerations
Recent changes may not be accessible on a standby during lag or a primary failure due to asynchronous replication. Before depending on replicas, I would keep SwiftBank balance-changing transactions on the primary and keep an eye on the health of the replicas. Although synchronous replication can increase latency and make writes dependent on standby availability, it may lessen the chance of losing acknowledged commits. To avoid two writable primaries, I would need a tried-and-true failover procedure.

As data grows, sharding may be used to distribute customers or accounts according to a stable customer/account key. Atomic money movement would become more complicated if transfers between shards required coordination on both sides. I didn't use cloud deployment or sharding.

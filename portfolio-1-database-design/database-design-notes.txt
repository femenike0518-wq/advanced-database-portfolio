SwiftBank Database Design Notes

1. Case study
SwiftBank is a fictional banking system for the MIT 8103 Advanced Database Systems practical. Customers hold savings or current accounts and use them for deposits, withdrawals and transfers. Bank staff and administrators maintain customer/account records and review transaction history. The database stores customer contact details, account identifiers and balances, and transactions with type, amount, status, date and account references.

2. Relational schema
- customers: customer_id is the primary key. full_name, email, phone and created_at describe a customer. email is unique and required.
- accounts: account_id is the primary key; customer_id is a foreign key to customers.customer_id. account_number is unique and required. account_type is limited to savings/current. balance cannot be negative.
- transactions: transaction_id is the primary key. source_account_id and destination_account_id are nullable foreign keys to accounts.account_id. transaction_type, amount, status and transaction_date describe the event. The amount must be positive and status must be pending, completed or failed.

3. Relationships and transaction constraints
A customer may own multiple accounts, while each account belongs to one customer. Transactions reference accounts in source and destination roles. A deposit requires a destination and has a NULL source. A withdrawal requires a source and has a NULL destination. A transfer requires both account references and they must be different. These conditions are enforced by chk_transaction_accounts, in addition to the foreign keys.

4. Seed data and balances
The seed script includes six fictional customers, six accounts, opening-balance deposits, and examples of completed deposits, withdrawals and transfers plus one failed withdrawal. The opening deposits equal the starting account balances. The completed later activity is balanced to net to zero, so stored balances remain equal to total completed deposits/credits minus completed withdrawals/debits. The failed attempt does not move money. The original three customer/account records are retained to match the MongoDB portfolio.

5. Keys, constraints and normalisation
Primary keys identify rows; foreign keys preserve relationships; unique email and account number constraints prevent duplicates. CHECK constraints restrict account type, nonnegative balances, positive amounts, allowed status/type values and valid source/destination combinations.

The schema follows 1NF by storing atomic values and using rows rather than repeating account columns. Each table has a single-column primary key and its non-key fields describe that row (2NF for this design). Customer, account and transaction facts are separated into their own tables and linked by foreign keys rather than repeated (3NF design rationale). A formal normal-form proof would require enumerating all functional dependencies and candidate keys.

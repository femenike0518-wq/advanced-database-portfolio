SwiftBank Database Design Notes

1. Case study
For the MIT 8103 Advanced Database Systems practical, SwiftBank is a fictional banking system. Customers use their current or savings accounts to make deposits, withdrawals and transfers. Customer account records and transaction history are kept up to date by bank employees and administrators. Customer contact information, account numbers, balances and transactions with type, amount, status, date and account references are all stored in the database.

2. Relational schema
- customers: The primary key for customers is customer_id. A customer is identified by their full name, email, phone number and created_at. Email is special and necessary.
- accounts: The primary key for accounts is account_id; the foreign key for customers is  customer_id. customer_id and account_number are distinct and necessary. The only available account types are savings and current. Negative balance is not possible
- transactions: The primary key for transactions is transaction_id, while the nullable foreign keys to accounts.account_id are source_account_id and destination_account_id. The event is described by transaction_type, amount, status and transaction_date. The status must be pending, finished, or unsuccessful and the amount must be positive.

3. Relationships and transaction constraints
A customer may own more than one account, and each account belongs to one customer. Accounts in source and destination roles are referenced in transactions. A deposit has a NULL source and needs a destination. A withdrawal has a NULL destination and needs a source. Both account references are needed for a transfer and they have to be distinct. Along with the foreign keys, chk_transaction_accounts enforces these requirements.

4. Seed data and balances
Six fictitious customers, six accounts, opening-balance deposits, completed deposits, withdrawals and transfers as well as one unsuccessful withdrawal, are all included in the seed script. The initial account balances are equal to the opening deposits. Stored balances are equal to the sum of all completed deposits and credits less all completed withdrawals and debits because the completed later activity is balanced to net to zero. Money is not moved by the unsuccessful attempt. To match the MongoDB portfolio, the initial three customer/account records are kept.

5. Keys, constraints and normalization
Rows are identified by primary keys, relationships are maintained by foreign keys and duplicates are avoided by unique email addresses and account numbers. Account type, non-negative balances, positive amounts, permitted status/type values and acceptable source/destination combinations are all limited by CHECK constraints.

By using rows instead of repeating account columns and storing atomic values, the schema adheres to 1NF. Every table has a single-column primary key and each row us described by its non-key fields (2NF for this design). Instead of being repeated, customer, account and transaction facts are divided into separate tables and connected by foreign keys (3NF design rationale). Listing every functional dependency and potential key would be necessary for a formal normal-form proof.
